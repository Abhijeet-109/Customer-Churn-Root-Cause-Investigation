# 07 — Root Cause Impact Ranking

## Objective

The identified churn drivers were ranked to determine which factors have the greatest overall impact on customer churn.

The ranking considers both the **strength of the churn relationship** and the **number of customers affected**.

---

## Impact Ranking

| Rank | Root Cause | Evidence | Impact |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Service Quality | Highest recorded churn reason and strong relationship with response time and satisfaction | **High** |
| 2 | Product Issues | Churn increases consistently as product issues increase | **High** |
| 3 | Pricing | Higher-charge customers show elevated churn | **Medium–High** |
| 4 | Competition | High competitive exposure is associated with significantly higher churn | **Medium** |

---

## 1. Service Quality — Highest Impact

Service quality has the strongest overall impact because it combines a high number of affected churned customers with clear differences in customer experience.

Customers facing longer response times and lower satisfaction show higher churn.

The recorded churn reasons also show **Service Quality as the largest category at 31.9%**.

**Priority:** Immediate

---

## 2. Product Issues — High Impact

Product issues show one of the clearest relationships with churn.

Customers with increasing numbers of product issues have progressively higher churn rates. This suggests that repeated product problems can significantly increase the likelihood of customer departure.

**Priority:** Immediate

---

## 3. Pricing — Medium–High Impact

Pricing contributes to churn, particularly among customers with higher monthly charges.

However, the relationship is not strong enough to conclude that pricing alone caused the overall increase.

Pricing should therefore be addressed alongside service and product improvements rather than treated as the single primary cause.

**Priority:** High

---

## 4. Competition — Medium Impact

Competitive pressure becomes particularly important for customers with high competitor exposure.

The high-exposure group has a churn rate of **19.4%**, compared with approximately **11–12%** for lower exposure groups.

Competition therefore represents a meaningful retention risk, especially for vulnerable customer segments.

**Priority:** Targeted

---

## Overall Impact Assessment

The analysis indicates that the churn increase is primarily associated with **customer experience and product-related problems**, with pricing and competitive pressure acting as additional contributors.

The ranking provides a practical basis for prioritizing retention initiatives:

```text
Service Quality
      ↓
Product Issues
      ↓
Pricing
      ↓
Competition
```

The next consideration is the financial impact of these churn drivers and the amount of revenue associated with lost customers.
